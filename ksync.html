The one-off trap

Syncing video with lighting, sound, and hardware usually ends one of two ways. You buy a proprietary player that only plays video, then pay for a separate show-control system and a technician's day rate to make the two talk to the rest of the room. Or you build it yourself, in QLab, again and again, a thousand euros of licences and weeks of work for a rig that only runs your one show.

Every ambitious venue lands in the same loop. kSync is a box that closes it, once: hardware your own team can keep running, priced for institutions that don't have an enterprise AV budget.

In the Wild

kSync ran the show floor for Johannes Büttner's Proof of Work, an expanded cinema installation on Bitcoin activists in Nairobi, staged at bauhaus reuse on Ernst-Reuter-Platz, Berlin, in 2025. Three metal-clad viewing stations, wired with salvaged monitors, spin into sync at the end of each loop. One timeline held eleven displays and the kinetic rotation together for the run of the show.

Built in Berlin

kSync is designed and built by Grayson Earle, a media artist whose work has shown at the Whitney, KW Institute, and transmediale. He needed this tool for his own exhibition first. The first version ran his own work at KW in 2023. That's why the hardware is yours to keep, with no licence fees or subscriptions inflating the price for the institutions kSync was made for.

Bootstrapped on purpose: hardware sales fund the company, and every unit is designed and assembled by hand in Berlin, so a small gallery and a large museum can deploy the same box.
