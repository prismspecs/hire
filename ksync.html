The one-off trap

Syncing video with lighting, sound, and hardware usually ends one of two ways. You pay €500 to €8,000+ per node for a proprietary player, plus a technician's day rate to make it talk to the rest of the room. Or you build it yourself, in QLab, again and again, a thousand euros of licences and weeks of work for a rig that only runs your one show.

Every ambitious venue lands in the same loop. kSync is a box that closes it, once: hardware your own team can keep running, priced for institutions that don't have an enterprise AV budget.

Built in Berlin

kSync is designed and built by Grayson Earle, a media artist whose work has shown at the Whitney, KW Institute, and transmediale. He needed this tool for his own exhibition first. That's why it's yours to keep, no licence fees, no subscriptions, nothing to inflate the price for the institutions kSync was made for.

Bootstrapped on purpose: hardware sales fund the company, European design with local German manufacturing, so a small gallery and a large museum can deploy the same box.
